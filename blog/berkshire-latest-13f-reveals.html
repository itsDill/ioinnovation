Berkshire’s June 2026 13F: top holdings and concentration

Published: August 18, 2026 · Updated: October 20, 2026 · By IO Innovation

Berkshire Hathaway Inc.’s report for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 listed 89 reportable holdings with a combined reported value of about $299.3 billion. The five largest issuer-level positions account for roughly 71.9% of that reported value. That is a useful concentration snapshot—not a complete picture of Berkshire’s assets, current trades, or investment rationale.

A Form 13F is filed after the quarter closes, so this report describes holdings as of June 30—not what Berkshire held on the August filing date or today. The snapshot is useful for studying disclosed long positions and concentration, but it cannot identify the exact day a position changed or explain why.

What the concentration does—and doesn’t—say

Apple is the largest issuer-level position in this snapshot at about 22.0% of reported 13F value. American Express is next at 17.1%; together, those two account for roughly 39.2% of the reported total. The top-five figure makes clear that the disclosed securities are concentrated in a handful of companies.

This is not the same as saying those positions make up 71.9% of Berkshire Hathaway’s entire balance sheet or total investment portfolio. Form 13F covers specified reportable securities under investment discretion; it does not provide a full balance sheet, cash balance, private-company holdings, or a complete view of short exposure. Nor does a large reported position, by itself, establish a manager’s current view or future action.

Reported market value can move because of share-price changes even when the number of shares is unchanged. To investigate a possible add or trim, compare the reported share counts for the same issuer and class across consecutive filings, account for splits or other corporate actions, and verify the underlying SEC documents. A single quarter is not enough to infer motive or “conviction.”

A practical way to read the next 13F

  1. Start with the report date. Treat it as a quarter-end snapshot, not a real-time portfolio.
  2. Compare shares, not just dollar values. Price movements affect reported value and weight.
  3. Aggregate carefully. One issuer may appear in multiple classes or line items.
  4. Check the source filing. Confirm issuer, class, share count, and reported value in the SEC document.
  5. Keep the scope in view. 13F is one disclosure window into reportable securities—not the whole business or a recommendation to copy a trade.
